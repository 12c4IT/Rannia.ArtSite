# Proposed Case Studies

A starting slate of eight artists for the v1 launch, spread across the four frames as primary lenses and balanced between Australian and international practice. **This is a proposal — Joe makes the final call.**

All artists below are very well-documented in published gallery materials, artist statements, scholarly writing, and major museum collections, which makes sourcing achievable. Each entry includes notes on likely sources, the kind of content area coverage you'd get, and a couple of artwork candidates.

A note on this list: it leans toward the canon of NSW HSC case studies — the artists who appear in NESA exemplars, are well-represented in Australian public collections, and have stable, citable institutional writing about them. There are excellent artists not on this list. Joe should swap freely.

---

## Subjective frame

### 1. Louise Bourgeois (French-American, 1911–2010)

- **Why she fits the subjective frame**: her work is explicitly autobiographical, drawing on memory, trauma, the body, and the maternal. She has talked and written about this at length, so the subjective reading is well-supported by primary sources.
- **Content areas**: the body, memory, identity.
- **Candidate works** (verify rights before use):
  - *Maman* / *Spider* series (1996+)
  - *Cell* series
  - *The Destruction of the Father* (1974)
- **Sources to seed**: MoMA collection pages, Tate exhibition catalogues, the Easton Foundation (her estate), interview transcripts in *Bourgeois: Destruction of the Father / Reconstruction of the Father* (MIT Press).
- **Strong cross-frame readings**: cultural (psychoanalysis, gender), structural (recurring symbols — spider, spiral, cell), postmodern (her late-career embrace as a feminist icon).

### 2. Bill Henson (Australian, b. 1955)

- **Why he fits the subjective frame**: mood, atmosphere, adolescence, interiority, ambiguity — the work foregrounds the psychological and the emotional.
- **Content areas**: the body, time, memory, the everyday.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Untitled* series, 1985/86 or 2000/01 cibachromes (verify rights)
  - *Paris Opera Project* (1990–91)
- **Content warning**: some Henson series engage adolescent bodies; choose works that are uncontroversial for an HSC student audience. The 2008 controversy is its own teachable moment about the **audience** agency of the conceptual framework.
- **Sources to seed**: Art Gallery of NSW collection pages, MCA, *The Monthly* essays, Robert McFarlane criticism, Roslyn Oxley9 Gallery.

## Cultural frame

### 3. Tracey Moffatt (Australian, b. 1960)

- **Why she fits the cultural frame**: her work explicitly engages race, class, gender, settler colonialism, and the staging of identity in Australian visual culture.
- **Content areas**: identity, place, the everyday, memory.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Something More* (1989) — colour photographic series
  - *Up in the Sky* (1997) — black-and-white photographic series
  - *Scarred for Life* (1994)
- **Sources to seed**: Art Gallery of NSW, MCA, the National Portrait Gallery, Roslyn Oxley9, *Frieze* and *Artforum* features, NESA exemplar responses (Moffatt has been a perennial case study choice).
- **Cross-frame**: postmodern (cinematic staging, appropriation of melodrama tropes), structural (colour palette and composition as meaning-makers).

### 4. Brook Andrew (Wiradjuri, b. 1970)

- **Why he fits the cultural frame**: explicitly engages colonial archives, ethnographic photography, race, and the politics of looking. Works through Wiradjuri visual language alongside Western modernism.
- **Content areas**: identity, place, memory.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Sexy and Dangerous* (1996) — type C photograph
  - *Marks* installations
  - *The Cell* / *Theme Park* works
- **Cultural protocols**: defer to Andrew's own framing; check the artist's website and gallery materials.
- **Sources to seed**: MCA, NGA, Tolarno Galleries, Andrew's own statements, *un Magazine*, NAVA.

## Structural frame

### 5. Rosalie Gascoigne (New Zealand-Australian, 1917–1999)

- **Why she fits the structural frame**: an absolute pleasure for formal analysis — she works through repetition, grid, found surfaces, weathered colour, text-as-texture. The materials carry their own structural meaning (road signs, soft-drink crates, retro-reflective sheeting).
- **Content areas**: place (Monaro landscape), the everyday, objects, nature.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Monaro* (1989)
  - *Earth* (1999)
  - *Star Chart* (1995)
- **Sources to seed**: NGA collection page (extensive), AGNSW, *Rosalie Gascoigne: Plain Air* (Mary Eagle), Roslyn Oxley9.
- **Cross-frame**: subjective (her own relationship to the Monaro), cultural (postcolonial reading of found Australiana).

### 6. Anish Kapoor (British-Indian, b. 1954)

- **Why he fits the structural frame**: monumental scale, pure form, void/solid play, single-pigment surfaces, mirrored surfaces that interact with surrounding space. Structural reading is the natural anchor.
- **Content areas**: spirituality, the body (as displaced presence), place.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Cloud Gate* (2004) — Chicago
  - *Sky Mirror* (2001)
  - *Marsyas* (2002) — Tate Turbine Hall
  - *1000 Names* series (pigment works)
- **Sources to seed**: Tate, Royal Academy, Lisson Gallery, Kapoor's own statements (many published interviews).

## Postmodern frame

### 7. Yasumasa Morimura (Japanese, b. 1951)

- **Why he fits the postmodern frame**: textbook postmodern — appropriates canonical Western paintings (Velázquez, Manet, Vermeer) inserting himself into them as every figure. Plays with race, gender, the gaze, originality, art-historical authority.
- **Content areas**: identity, the body.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *Portrait (Futago)* (1988) — after Manet's *Olympia*
  - *Las Meninas Renacen de Noche* series (2013)
  - *Doublonnage (Marcel)* (1988)
- **Sources to seed**: SFMOMA, Andy Warhol Museum, Japan Society NYC, Luhring Augustine.
- **Cross-frame**: cultural (Japanese-Western art-historical encounter), subjective (self-presentation, masquerade).

### 8. Imants Tillers (Australian, b. 1950)

- **Why he fits the postmodern frame**: appropriation across his "Canvasboard" works (using individually-numbered canvas boards as a system), recombining motifs from Aboriginal artists (with controversy), German Romantics, Latvian heritage, art history at large. A foundational Australian postmodernist.
- **Content areas**: place, identity, memory.
- **Candidate works**:
  - *The Nine Shots* (1985) — significant and controversial
  - *Diaspora* (1992)
  - *Nature Speaks* series
- **Critical note**: the appropriation of Michael Nelson Jagamara's *Five Stories* in *The Nine Shots* is itself a major teachable controversy — about the postmodern frame and the cultural frame's limits. Worth engaging directly rather than tiptoeing around.
- **Sources to seed**: NGA, MCA, Roslyn Oxley9, *Imants Tillers: One World Many Visions* (NGA), Wayne Tunnicliffe's curatorial writing.
