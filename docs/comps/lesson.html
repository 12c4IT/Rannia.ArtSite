Reading Maman through the subjective frame

Learning intentions

Lesson

Biography, made monumental

Hook · 5 min

One image, one question

Project Maman at full Turbine-Hall scale. One prompt on the board: “Is this spider protecting you or trapping you?” Sixty seconds, no hands down.

Modelled · 15 min

A worked paragraph

Think aloud through one paragraph of subjective analysis, naming each move as you make it — claim, evidence, frame, link back to the artist.

Bourgeois described the spider as “an ode to my mother … like a spider, my mother was a weaver.” The marble egg-sac cradled beneath the abdomen makes the protective reading literal, while the thin steel legs enclose the viewer — the work holds tenderness and threat in the same body.
Guided · 15 min

Now the second artwork

In pairs, students attempt the same move on The Destruction of the Father. Circulate; push for the link between a material choice (latex, red light) and a psychological reading.

Independent · 10 min

Attempt the question

Students attempt the linked HSC question below. They may open the scaffold only after a first attempt.

Question scaffold

HSC 2019 · Section II · Question 9 25 marks

Analyse how an artist's choice of materials and processes can shape meaning in their work. In your response, refer to at least one artist you have studied.

Decode the question
  • Analyse — break the work into parts and show how they relate; don't just describe.
  • Materials and processes — the physical stuff and the making, not the subject matter.
  • Shape meaning — argue the causal link: this choice → this reading.
Plan your response
  • Intro: thesis — materials are not neutral; nominate the subjective frame and name Bourgeois.
  • Body 1: bronze, marble, steel in Maman — scale and tension.
  • Body 2: latex and red light in The Destruction of the Father.
  • Conclusion: return to the thesis — the choice is the content.
Sentence stems

“By casting the legs in visibly-seamed bronze, Bourgeois …”

“The shift from marble to latex registers as a shift from … to …”

“Read through the subjective frame, the material choice …”

Marker's view

Band 6: sustains a single argument, integrates the frame with close material analysis, uses precise vocabulary and cited evidence.

Band 4: names materials and the frame, but description outweighs analysis; the causal link stays implicit.
