Glossary

The words, defined

Terminology used across the case studies and frames. Each entry deep-links, so the analysis can point straight here.

ABCDEF GHIJKL MNOPQR STUVWXYZ

A

Appropriation

Taking an existing image, object or style and re-using it in a new work so that its original meaning is recast. A defining strategy of the postmodern frame.

Postmodern
See also motif, signifier

Autobiography

Art that draws directly on the artist's own life, family and memory as its subject matter — the engine of much work read through the subjective frame.

Subjective
See also motif

C

Colonialism

The historical and ongoing structures of settler and imperial power. A recurring concern of the cultural frame, especially in Australian and First Nations art.

Cultural
See also identity, representation

Composition

The arrangement of visual elements within a work — what is placed where, and the relationships between the parts. The bedrock of structural analysis.

Structural
See also scale

Context

The circumstances — historical, social, personal — in which a work is made and received. Meaning is never produced in a vacuum.

CulturalSubjective

G

The gaze

Who looks and who is looked at — the power relations built into the way a work positions its viewer, and whom it assumes that viewer to be.

CulturalPostmodern
See also representation

I

Identity

How a work constructs or questions selfhood — personal, cultural, national, gendered. A content area as well as a lens.

CulturalSubjective

Ideology

A system of beliefs and values — often unspoken — that a work can carry, naturalise, or set out to contest.

Cultural
See also representation

M

Materiality

The physical properties of a work's materials treated as carriers of meaning rather than neutral supports — latex reads differently from marble.

StructuralSubjective
See also scale

Mise-en-scène

Everything arranged within the frame of an image — setting, props, lighting, pose. Borrowed from film, indispensable for reading staged photography.

StructuralCultural

Motif

A recurring image, form or element that accrues meaning as it returns across a body of work — Bourgeois's spider, spiral and cell.

SubjectiveStructural

R

Representation

The way a work stands in for — and shapes our understanding of — people, places or ideas. Always a construction, never a neutral mirror.

CulturalPostmodern
See also the gaze, ideology

S

Scale

The size of a work relative to the human body, and the meaning that size produces — intimacy, intimidation, monumentality.

Structural
See also composition

Signifier

In semiotics, the form — an image, mark or object — that carries a meaning (the signified). The structural frame reads works as systems of signs.

StructuralPostmodern
See also appropriation
