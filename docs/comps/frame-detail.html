The cultural frame

The cultural frame reads a work through the social conditions that produced and received it — class, race, gender, ethnicity, politics, religion, and the particular time and place. Art is treated as embedded in society, not separate from it.

Key questions

What to ask a work through this frame

  1. What social, political or historical conditions shaped this work?
  2. Whose values does it carry — and whose does it challenge?
  3. How do class, race, gender or ethnicity operate within it?
  4. Who was it made for, and how did different audiences receive it?
  5. What does the work reveal about its own time and place?

Vocabulary

Ideology Representation Appropriation The gaze Colonialism Identity Context

The frames are a syllabus framework — defined once here, applied throughout the case studies. They are not mutually exclusive: most works reward more than one.
