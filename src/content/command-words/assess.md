---
term: "Assess"
slug: assess
alarmsRung: evaluate

definition: |
  Make a judgement of value, quality, outcomes, results, or size. Determine how well something meets its purpose.

studentGloss: |
  Weigh it up. "Assess" is close to "evaluate" but slightly softer — it wants your considered judgement about worth, quality, or effectiveness, not necessarily against explicit criteria. Say what's strong, say what's weak, and land on a position.

workedExample:
  question: "Assess the significance of Louise Bourgeois' 1982 MoMA retrospective to her subsequent career."
  markValue: 8
  response: |
    The 1982 MoMA retrospective is arguably the single most important event in Louise Bourgeois' recognition, and its significance can be assessed at three levels: institutional, biographical, and art-historical. Institutionally, it was the first full retrospective at the Museum of Modern Art of a woman artist, which meant the exhibition itself became a story — not just Bourgeois' work but the fact of the show attracted attention. Biographically, Bourgeois was seventy-one at the time. Recognition at that age is not the same event as recognition at forty; it converted four decades of relatively obscure practice into a legible narrative that curators, critics and collectors could then engage with.

    The strongest evidence for the retrospective's significance is what happened after it. In the eighteen years between 1982 and *Maman*'s installation at Tate Modern's opening in 2000, Bourgeois moved from a small-circle reputation to the position of one of the most-cited living artists internationally. That trajectory is not conceivable without the MoMA event as a hinge.

    A fair assessment must also name what the retrospective did not do. It did not immediately change the terms on which Bourgeois' work was received — the feminist critical vocabulary that eventually let her practice be read fully as *content* rather than as *biography* took another decade to develop. Weighing both, the retrospective's significance is high but not total: it was the institutional pre-condition for later recognition, but not the intellectual event that made the recognition legible.
  markersNote: |
    "Assess" asks for a considered judgement. This response earns the mark by naming the position early (arguably the single most important event), then giving the evidence for the judgement (three levels: institutional, biographical, art-historical), then qualifying it fairly (what the retrospective did not do). The qualifying move is important — a response that only praised the event without acknowledging its limits reads as promotional, not assessed.
