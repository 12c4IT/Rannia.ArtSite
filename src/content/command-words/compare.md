---
term: "Compare"
slug: compare
alarmsRung: explain

definition: |
  Show how things are similar or different. Give both the similarities and the differences — a comparison that only names one side is incomplete.

studentGloss: |
  Put two things next to each other and name what changes between them and what stays the same. **Both directions matter.** A response that only lists differences (or only similarities) is not a comparison — it's a description of two things beside each other.

workedExample:
  question: "Compare how two works by Louise Bourgeois use material to hold psychological content."
  markValue: 8
  response: |
    *Maman* (1999) and *The Destruction of the Father* (1974) both treat material as a container for psychological content, but they choose their materials for opposite reasons. In *Maman*, Bourgeois uses bronze — the most orthodox material in Western public sculpture — precisely because it *is* the material of the civic monument. The choice reads as strategic annexation: Bourgeois takes a material historically associated with male authority and uses it to house the maternal. The permanence of bronze is doing meaning-work; the mother is memorialised in the same material as the general on horseback.

    *The Destruction of the Father* uses latex, plaster and coloured light — soft, temporary, medical materials with no fine-art tradition in 1974. The choice reads as rejection: Bourgeois refuses the sculptural materials of her male minimalist contemporaries in favour of materials coded as bodily and interior. Where *Maman*'s bronze insists on permanence, *Destruction*'s latex insists on transience — the emotional content of the work is bodily and unstable, and its material shares that quality.

    What both works share is the argument that material is never neutral. In both, Bourgeois is choosing not just *what to make the work of* but *what the material means in the wider culture* — and using that meaning to do argumentative work.
  markersNote: |
    A good "compare" response moves in both directions — it names similarities AND differences. This response earns the mark by opening on a shared move (both use material to hold psychological content), then working through the differences (permanent bronze vs. transient latex; annexation vs. rejection), then closing on a deeper shared move (material is never neutral). A response that only listed differences would be incomplete for the verb.
