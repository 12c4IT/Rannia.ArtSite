---
term: "Evaluate"
slug: evaluate
alarmsRung: evaluate

definition: |
  Make a judgement based on criteria; determine the value of. Judge to what extent something is effective or successful.

studentGloss: |
  Make a judgement, and back it up with reasons that show you know the criteria. "Evaluate" is different from "describe" or "explain" — the marker wants your **verdict**. Don't sit on the fence; a strong response takes a position and defends it.

workedExample:
  question: "Evaluate the role of scale in the practice of one artist you have studied."
  markValue: 10
  response: |
    Scale is central — not incidental — to Louise Bourgeois' practice, and the strongest evidence for this is that Bourgeois calibrates scale differently for different psychological content. In *Maman* (1999), Bourgeois works at civic scale (9.27 metres) because the work has to move a private image (her mother) into public space. The scale is not an aesthetic preference; it is the mechanism that does the argumentative work. Without the shift to monumental bronze, *Maman* would remain a personal memorial, not a public reversal of who monuments are for.

    In *The Destruction of the Father* (1974), by contrast, Bourgeois works at domestic scale, roughly the volume of a family dinner scene. The choice is again content-driven: the fantasy has to be legible as a *family* fantasy, not a public monument. If Bourgeois had scaled the work to civic size, its psychological register would have collapsed.

    Judged against the criterion of *effectiveness for content*, then, Bourgeois' calibration of scale is one of the most consistently successful moves in late twentieth-century sculpture. Where a contemporary like Anish Kapoor uses monumental scale as a stable signature (see *Cloud Gate*, 2004), Bourgeois lets each work choose its own scale from the content upward. That responsiveness is what makes scale, in her practice, an evaluated aesthetic decision rather than a house style.
  markersNote: |
    "Evaluate" wants a judgement and a criterion. This response gives both explicitly: the judgement is that scale is *central* and successful, and the criterion is *fitness for psychological content*. It also earns marks by comparing against another artist (Kapoor) — the comparison sharpens the evaluation. A candidate who only describes Bourgeois' scale without making a judgement misses the mark of the verb.
