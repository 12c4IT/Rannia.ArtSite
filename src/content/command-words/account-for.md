---
term: "Account for"
slug: account-for
alarmsRung: explain

definition: |
  State the reasons for something. Explain the causes of a situation, event, or condition.

studentGloss: |
  Give the *reasons*. "Account for" is close to "explain" but leans harder on **causes**. Why did this happen? Why did the artist make this choice? Your response is a set of connected reasons, not a description.

workedExample:
  question: "Account for the recognition Louise Bourgeois received in the final three decades of her career, given her relative obscurity for the four decades before."
  markValue: 8
  response: |
    Louise Bourgeois' late recognition can be accounted for by three connected shifts in the art world she was making work inside. First, the 1982 MoMA retrospective — curated by Deborah Wye — placed Bourgeois in front of a mainstream American audience for the first time. She was seventy-one, and the first woman to receive a full MoMA retrospective. That institutional recognition converted a small-circle reputation into a national one.

    Second, the rise of feminist art criticism in the 1970s and 80s gave a critical vocabulary for the maternal, domestic, and biographical content Bourgeois had been working with for decades. Where minimalism could not "see" her work, feminism could. Bourgeois' subject matter — the maternal body, paternal authority, the domestic — became legible as serious art rather than as sentimental or private.

    Third, the installation-driven contemporary art of the 1990s and 2000s (from Christian Boltanski to Rachel Whiteread) made Bourgeois' Cells and immersive environments read as anticipations of a now-central genre. Bourgeois was no longer out of step; the field had moved to where she had been for forty years.
  markersNote: |
    "Account for" asks for reasons. This response gives three, and they are *connected* — each shift builds on the previous. A weaker response would simply list events (MoMA 1982, feminism, installation art) without saying how each reason contributed to the outcome. Naming the reasons is not enough; the marker is looking for the causal chain.
