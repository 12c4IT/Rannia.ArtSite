---
term: "Analyse"
slug: analyse
alarmsRung: analyse

definition: |
  Identify components and the relationship between them; draw out and relate implications. Ask *why* each of the ideas or components interact.

studentGloss: |
  Break the work into its parts. Say what each part is doing. Then say how the parts **work on each other** to make meaning. If you only describe, you have not analysed — analysis is the *relational* move between the parts.

workedExample:
  question: "Analyse how one artist you have studied uses scale to construct meaning."
  markValue: 8
  response: |
    Louise Bourgeois uses scale in *Maman* (1999) as a compositional device that produces meaning through the viewer's body. The bronze spider stands 9.27 metres tall — a scale that forces adult viewers to physically position themselves beneath it in order to see the sac of marble eggs suspended from its abdomen. The relationship Bourgeois analyses through scale is therefore not just formal; it is bodily. The adult viewer is placed in the position of a small child looking up at an enormous maternal figure.

    Scale also transforms Bourgeois' material. Cast bronze at nine metres does not read as insect but as architecture — a maternal shelter under which the viewer is briefly held. The shift from the small (a real spider) to the monumental (a public sculpture) is what allows Bourgeois to move the work from private biography into public monument. Scale, in *Maman*, is not decoration — it is the mechanism through which the sculpture works on its audience.
  markersNote: |
    The response earns the mark because it doesn't stop at description. It names a component (scale), says what that component does (positions the viewer bodily), and then relates that back to meaning (private image made public monument). That third move — relating the component to meaning — is where "analyse" lives. Description alone would land in Band 4.
