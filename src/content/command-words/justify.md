---
term: "Justify"
slug: justify
alarmsRung: evaluate

definition: |
  Support an argument or conclusion. Show why your position is defensible against the alternatives.

studentGloss: |
  Take a position and defend it. "Justify" wants you to name a claim and then give the reasons that make the claim stronger than the reasonable alternatives. You are not just describing what happened — you are arguing for one reading over another.

workedExample:
  question: "Justify the view that Louise Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999) is a work of feminist art, despite the artist's own reluctance to be labelled a feminist."
  markValue: 10
  response: |
    Louise Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999) is a work of feminist art in effect, if not in the artist's own self-identification, and the strongest evidence for this is what the sculpture does at the level of form. Bourgeois takes the material of civic bronze — historically the material of male authority, of generals and emperors on horseback — and uses it to build a monument to the maternal. A subject that art history had kept small and domestic (mother, child, sewing) is placed at the scale of the civic monument. That formal move is a feminist argument, whether or not Bourgeois would call it one.

    An honest justification also names the counter-position: Bourgeois herself resisted the feminist label, preferring to describe her work as personal and psychoanalytic rather than political. This is a real objection and should not be dismissed. But intention is not the only criterion for whether a work is feminist. If a sculpture succeeds in reversing the historical gendering of monumental form, it does feminist work regardless of the maker's self-description. The work argues for the reading even when the artist argues against it.

    Finally, the reception of *Maman* by feminist critics — Marina Warner, Griselda Pollock, and others — has been sustained enough to constitute a critical tradition. If a work is read as feminist by two generations of feminist critics, and if the formal moves justify that reading against alternatives, then the claim stands.
  markersNote: |
    "Justify" wants a defended position. This response earns the mark by naming the position early ("is a work of feminist art in effect"), acknowledging the strongest counter-argument (Bourgeois' self-description), and giving formal and reception-based reasons for holding the position anyway. A response that only asserts the position, or only lists Bourgeois' quotes, would miss the *defensive* quality of the verb.
