scaffold:
  decode: |
    "Discuss" wants movement — arguments on both sides. The prompt gives you a claim; agreeing wholesale is easier but weaker. A better response holds the claim, tests it against a limit case, then lands somewhere considered.
  plan: |
    Three paragraphs. Paragraph 1: argue for the claim using one artwork — show how two frames together produce a stronger reading than either alone. Paragraph 2: complicate the claim — note the risk of frame-mixing (loss of analytical sharpness, or reading one frame's evidence into another's argument). Paragraph 3: land the position — under what conditions frame-mixing works, under what conditions it collapses.
  sentenceStems:
    - "The claim holds most clearly when we look at [work]..."
    - "But there is a risk: if the frames are not held distinct, [problem]..."
    - "The strongest responses therefore..."
  markersView: |
    Band 4-5: uses two frames on one work.
    Band 5-6: uses two frames AND shows why using both is stronger than one.
    Band 6: also names the limit — when moving between frames stops helping and starts confusing the argument. That third move earns the top band.
