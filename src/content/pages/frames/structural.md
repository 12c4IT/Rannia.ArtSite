## What the structural frame is

The structural frame reads a work through **its formal and material make-up**: composition, colour, line, shape, texture, scale, medium, and the codes and conventions that make signs legible as art. It treats every choice the artist makes as carrying meaning, and asks how the work's form constructs its content.

## Questions this frame asks

- What is the work made of, and what does the choice of material contribute?
- How are the formal elements — colour, line, shape, composition, scale — organised, and to what effect?
- What signs and symbols does the work deploy, and what codes make them legible?
- What conventions of the medium is the work following, adapting, or breaking?

## Worked example — Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999)

**Question**: Analyse how the structural frame reads Louise Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999).

Structurally, *Maman* resolves an old sculptural problem: how to make a heavy material feel weightless. The choice of bronze is orthodox — bronze has been the material of public monuments for millennia. What Bourgeois does with it is not. The legs are elongated far past what bronze normally needs, tapered to points that read almost as calligraphic marks against the ground plane. The result is a sculpture that occupies enormous volume while touching the earth at only eight points.

The egg sac introduces the second material — a stainless steel mesh cage holding twenty-six polished marble eggs. Marble against bronze against steel: three distinct material vocabularies compressed into a single work. The whiteness of the eggs, the darkness of the bronze, and the industrial grey of the mesh set up a chromatic hierarchy that resolves at the sculpture's most tender point.

Scale, formally, is a device rather than a decoration. The spider is not merely large — it is the exact size at which an adult viewer must physically position themselves beneath it to see the eggs. Formalism here is not surface arrangement; it is the way the sculpture *organises the viewer's body*.

**Marker's note**: The structural frame is about how *form* constructs meaning. This response earns the mark by naming specific structural components (material, scale, negative space, chromatic hierarchy) and saying what each contributes. A weaker response would describe the sculpture's appearance without connecting appearance to argument. Every formal observation here has a "so what" attached.
