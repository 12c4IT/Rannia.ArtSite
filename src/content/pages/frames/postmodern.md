## What the postmodern frame is

The postmodern frame reads a work through its **strategies of appropriation, irony, parody, recontextualisation, and the blurring of high and low culture**. It treats originality as suspect, meaning as unstable, and the artist as a re-arranger of pre-existing images and codes. It asks how a work interrogates the categories — the frame itself included — that would try to contain it.

## Questions this frame asks

- What existing images, works, or codes is this work quoting, appropriating, or transforming?
- How does the work use irony, parody, pastiche, or self-reference?
- What "high" or "low" cultural categories is the work moving between?
- What claims about originality, authorship, or authenticity is the work interrogating?

## Worked example — Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999)

**Question**: Discuss how the postmodern frame reads Louise Bourgeois' *Maman* (1999).

Read through the postmodern frame, *Maman* becomes visible as a citation — a knowing quotation of every monumental bronze animal in Western public art history, from Rome's Capitoline she-wolf to the many bronze lions guarding civic buildings. Bourgeois quotes the tradition and inverts it. The animal is not male; it is not heroic; it is not a symbol of civic power. It is a mother, and its power is generative rather than martial.

The multiplicity of casts is itself a postmodern gesture. Copies exist in Tate Modern, Guggenheim Bilbao, Ottawa, Tokyo, Seoul, Doha, and Kansas City. There is no *original* *Maman*. The work exists as a distributed presence — each cast the "same" work, each cast in a different city, none more authentic than the others. Bourgeois plays with — and against — the modernist cult of the unique original.

But a purely postmodern reading also flattens something. The affective register of *Maman* — the way viewers meeting the work in a plaza before knowing its history often report an immediate emotional response — is not just citational noise. The postmodern frame reveals what the subjective frame ignores (Bourgeois' knowing citation of a tradition); the subjective frame reveals what the postmodern frame flattens (the work's emotional force). The two frames read best when used together.

**Marker's note**: The postmodern frame is not a wildcard. It has specific analytic moves: appropriation, irony, questioning of originality, blurring of high/low, dispersal of authorship. This response earns the mark by naming two of them (citation of tradition, multiplicity of casts) and — importantly — by acknowledging the limits of the frame ("a purely postmodern reading also flattens something").
